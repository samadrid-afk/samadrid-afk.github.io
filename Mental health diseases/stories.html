Lena Crosswell

I used to think depression was just a word people threw around when they were sad, but living with it felt more like being slowly erased; days blurred together, and even the smallest tasks felt like climbing something endless, like I was stuck inside my own version of Sisyphus pushing a weight that always rolled back down. I’d wake up already tired, already behind, wondering how everyone else made life look so effortless while I was just trying to feel anything at all, and for a long time I didn’t tell anyone because I couldn’t explain it without sounding ungrateful or broken—but writing it here feels like the first honest thing I’ve done in a while, like maybe naming it is a way of reminding myself I’m still here.

Mara Ellington

Some days I feel like I’ve been plugged directly into lightning—thoughts racing faster than I can catch them, ideas stacking on top of each other until I’m convinced I can reinvent my whole life overnight—but just as quickly, it all collapses into a heaviness that makes even getting out of bed feel impossible, like I’m living between two extremes I never chose; I later learned to call it Bipolar Disorder, but for the longest time it just felt like I was either “too much” or “not enough,” swinging between a version of me that scared people with her intensity and one that disappeared completely, and writing this now is my way of trying to understand where one ends and the other begins, or if they’re both just parts of me I’m still learning how to hold together.

Mason Riley Brooks

I guess I’ve never really said this all out loud before, but my name is Mason Riley Brooks, and I’ve been dealing with schizophrenia for a few years now—it started as little things, like hearing my name when no one was around or feeling like people were watching me, and I brushed it off as stress, but it got worse, like voices that didn’t belong to anyone I knew and thoughts that didn’t feel like mine, and it honestly scared me because I couldn’t tell what was real anymore; some days I’m okay and can ground myself, especially with meds and therapy, but other days it feels like my brain is working against me, and even simple things like going outside or talking to people can feel overwhelming, so I’m here just trying to connect with anyone who understands what it’s like to live with this and still keep going.

Julian Chase Harper

I don’t usually post stuff like this, but my name’s Julian Chase Harper, and I’ve been dealing with PTSD for a while now—it didn’t hit me all at once, more like it crept in after everything happened, with flashbacks that come out of nowhere and dreams that don’t really feel like dreams, just reliving things I wish I could forget; sometimes it’s the smallest triggers that set it off, like a sound or a smell, and suddenly I’m not where I am anymore, and it makes it hard to relax or trust that I’m actually safe, even when I know I am, but I’ve been trying to work through it with therapy and just taking things one day at a time, and I guess I’m here to see if anyone else gets what that’s like.

Nathaniel Scott Hayes

I’ve been dealing with depression for longer than I like to admit—it’s not always this dramatic, obvious thing, sometimes it’s just waking up already exhausted or feeling nothing at all, like everything’s muted and distant, and even things I used to enjoy don’t really hit the same anymore; there are days where getting out of bed feels like a huge win and others where I just kind of drift through everything on autopilot, and it’s hard to explain to people because from the outside it probably looks like I’m fine, but I’ve been trying to manage it, little by little, and I guess I’m posting here to see if anyone else knows that feeling of just trying to keep going even when it’s heavy all the time.

Dominic Reid Sullivan

I’ve got PTSD. For me it’s less about fear all the time and more like my brain refuses to file certain things away, so they just replay whenever they feel like it, sometimes triggered, sometimes completely random. I’ve gotten pretty good at functioning on the outside—work, conversations, all that—but there’s always this underlying tension, like I’m half-waiting for something to go wrong. I cope in my own ways, some healthier than others, and I’ve learned how to ride out the rough moments without letting them completely take over, but it’s still tiring in a way that’s hard to explain unless you’ve lived it, so yeah, just putting this out there to see who else gets it.
