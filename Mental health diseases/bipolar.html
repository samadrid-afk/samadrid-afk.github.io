What is it?

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition that causes unusual shifts in a person's mood, energy, activity levels, and ability to function. People with bipolar disorder experience episodes of mania or hypomania periods of unusually elevated, irritable, or energetic mood—along with episodes of depression, which involve sadness, low energy, and loss of interest in activities. These mood episodes can last days, weeks, or longer and may affect sleep, decision-making, and relationships. The exact cause isn't fully known, but it is believed to involve a combination of genetics, brain chemistry, and environmental factors. Treatment typically includes medication, therapy, and lifestyle management to help stabilize mood and maintain daily functioning.

Getting help for Bipolar Disorder usually involves a combination of professional treatment, supportive habits, and a strong support system. The first step is to talk to a mental health professional such as a psychiatrist, psychologist, or licensed therapist who can provide an accurate diagnosis and create a treatment plan, which often includes therapy and mood-stabilizing medications. Keeping a regular routine—consistent sleep, balanced meals, exercise, and stress management—can help stabilize mood swings. Learning about the condition, tracking mood changes, and avoiding alcohol or drugs can also reduce triggers. Support from trusted friends, family, or support groups can make a big difference, and if symptoms become severe or include thoughts of self-harm, seeking immediate help from a crisis hotline or medical professional is important.
