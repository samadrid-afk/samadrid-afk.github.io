What is it?

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a mental health condition that can develop after a person experiences or witnesses a traumatic event, such as violence, a serious accident, natural disaster, or combat. People with PTSD may continue to feel intense fear or stress long after the danger has passed. Common symptoms include intrusive memories or flashbacks of the event, nightmares, avoidance of reminders of the trauma, negative changes in mood or thinking, and heightened reactions such as being easily startled or constantly on guard. PTSD can affect daily life, relationships, and sleep, but with treatments like therapy and sometimes medication, many people can manage symptoms and recover over time.

Fighting Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) usually involves a combination of professional help, daily coping strategies, and patience with your own healing process. Evidence-based treatments like trauma-focused therapy (such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy or Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) can help retrain how the brain processes traumatic memories, while healthy routines—regular exercise, good sleep, mindfulness or breathing practices, and staying connected with supportive people—help calm the nervous system. Some people also benefit from medications prescribed by a doctor, but many find that building coping skills, gradually facing triggers in a safe way, and practicing self-compassion are key steps in reducing symptoms and regaining a sense of control over their lives.
